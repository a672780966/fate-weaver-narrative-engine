# Research Questions

## Main Question

Can long-form narrative emerge from simulated emotional causality rather than direct plot generation?

## Hypotheses

### H1: Emotion-triggered memory activation improves psychological continuity.

Characters whose memories are activated by emotional and environmental state will appear more continuous than characters whose memories are retrieved only by semantic similarity.

### H2: Observer-style causal compression improves long-range narrative coherence.

Summaries that track causal turning points, emotional thresholds, and relationship shifts will help later scenes remain coherent better than chronological summaries alone.

### H3: Fate intervention is more believable when it amplifies existing character tendencies.

Readers will perceive interventions as less forced when they follow measurable internal drift.

### H4: Narrative selection is distinct from simulation logging.

A system that separates simulated events from reader-facing scenes will produce less diary-like, more literary output.

## Evaluation Ideas

- Compare semantic-memory agents against emotion-triggered-memory agents.
- Ask readers to rate character continuity across repeated scenes.
- Track whether later behavior can be traced to prior emotional thresholds.
- Compare chronological summaries against Observer causal indexes.
- Measure forced-plot perception in different intervention styles.
